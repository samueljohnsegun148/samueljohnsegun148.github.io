## The Problem

GPS works beautifully — until you walk through the front door. For people who are blind or have low vision, that's where navigation ends and anxiety begins.

Indoor spaces were never designed with blind and low-vision users in mind. When they get disoriented in an unfamiliar building — a hospital, a campus, a public facility — there's no reliable way to recover. Even well-meaning human assistance often makes things worse: directions like "go past the blue sign, turn left at the desk" use visual references that are meaningless to someone who can't see.

The consequence? Many avoid unfamiliar places altogether, giving up independence to avoid distress.

## The Insight

**The real problem isn't getting lost. It's the inability to recover.**

Most indoor navigation research focuses on preventing disorientation — better maps, better positioning. But prevention fails eventually. What happens then? That recovery moment — when someone is lost, anxious, and receiving unhelpful guidance — is where the system fails most critically.

## The Solution

I'm building a smartphone-based conversational assistant that does three things:

1. **Detects** when a blind user has become disoriented inside an unfamiliar building
2. **Assesses** their stress and anxiety level in real time
3. **Adapts** calm, contextual recovery guidance until they regain confidence and orientation

This isn't generic turn-by-turn directions. It's recovery support tailored to how lost and how anxious the user is, delivered through natural conversation.

## Why This Matters

- **Autonomy**: Every place a blind person avoids is a piece of independence lost
- **Health**: Navigation anxiety has real physiological and psychological effects
- **Safety**: In emergency situations (hospital evacuations, fire), the inability to navigate indoors becomes a survival issue
- **Equity**: Outdoor navigation was solved years ago. Indoor navigation for vulnerable populations remains unsolved

## Research Areas

- Disorientation detection in indoor environments
- Real-time anxiety and stress assessment
- Adaptive conversational guidance systems
- Human-centered AI for accessibility
- Indoor positioning and wayfinding recovery
